Hypotheses of the screened families (do not repeat them):

- E-001 VWAP reclaim: After the price stays on one side of the session VWAP for a while, the first close back across it marks a change of control; follow the cross.
- E-002 Previous-day high/low rejection: Trading beyond the previous session's high/low and closing back inside it is a failed auction at a level many participants watch; fade it.
- E-003 Previous-day high/low breakout: A close beyond the previous session's high/low triggers stops and breakout orders; follow it.
- E-004 Trend-day pullback to the EMA: On a day already moving more than usual for the time of day, the first pullback to a short EMA that closes back on the trend side is a continuation entry.
- E-005 Inside-bar breakout: A bar inside the previous one compresses the range; a close beyond it releases it in that direction.
- E-006 Opening-range failure: When the price breaks the opening range and then closes back inside it, the breakout traders are trapped; fade back into the range.
- E-007 Quiet continuation: After a large move, a bar in the move's direction on LOW volume means nobody is pushing back; follow.
- E-008 Midday extreme fade: Between 12:00 and 14:30 liquidity is thinner and breaks of the recent high/low tend to fail; fade a bar that pokes beyond and closes back inside.
- E-009 Pin-bar rejection: A bar of at least typical range with a small body and a long wick on one side shows that aggressive orders pushed the price there and were absorbed by resting liquidity; the absorbed side is exhausted, follow the close away from the wick.
- E-010 Strong-close bar follow: A bar of typical-or-larger range that is almost all body and closes at its extreme means aggressive orders were still being filled at the bell of the bar; a parent order still being worked continues into the next bars, so follow it.
- E-011 Break-and-retest: After a close beyond the recent high/low, a pullback that touches the broken level and closes back on the breakout side shows old resistance acting as support (late sellers covering, missed buyers stepping in); enter on the retest rather than the break.
- E-012 Close-location flow divergence: A new recent low while the volume-weighted close location of the same window is positive means bars keep closing high on volume: passive buyers absorb the sellers making the low; fade the extreme.
- E-013 Session extreme after a pause: When the session high/low has stood untouched for a long time, stops and breakout orders pile up beyond it; the first close through it after the pause releases them, follow the break.
- E-014 Engulfing reversal after a run: After several consecutive closes in one direction, a bar that reverses and closes beyond the previous bar's extreme shows the short-term momentum traders are trapped and the other side took control; follow the reversal.
- E-015 Late-day trend breakout: Late-informed traders and rebalancers finish their orders near the close in the direction of the day; in the last hour or two, on a day already moving more than usual, follow a break of the 15-minute high/low in the day's direction.
- E-016 Double test that holds: A return to the recent low (high) after moving away, that does not break it and closes in the bounce direction, shows resting orders still defend the level and sellers cannot push through; trade the hold.
- E-017 Flag after an impulse bar: A large impulse bar followed by small bars that hold its far half means the impulse side is not being unwound; a close beyond the impulse bar's extreme resumes the move as the pause traders give up.
- E-018 Swing-structure break: After a higher swing low (lower swing high), a close through the last swing high (low) completes an intraday trend structure that swing traders watch; their entries and the stops of the counter side push it on.
- E-019 Floor-pivot touch rejection: Floor-trader pivots (P, R1/S1, R2/S2 from yesterday's H/L/C) are plotted by most retail platforms, so take-profit and fresh limit orders rest there; a bar that reaches a pivot from one side and closes back on that side shows the resting orders absorbed the push, so trade away from the level.
- E-020 Range-exhaustion extreme fade: Once today's range already equals a large share of the 20-session average daily range, day traders' targets are met and fresh momentum buyers are scarce; a new session extreme made then is more likely to be the profit-takers' exit than a new leg, so fade it.
- E-021 Trend-filtered RSI(2) dip: Connors-style: inside a trend defined by a long intraday EMA, a 2-period RSI extreme against the trend is short-horizon liquidity selling (or buying) by impatient traders, which trend holders absorb; buy the dip above the EMA, sell the pop below it.
- E-022 Heavy-volume balance break: A window that trades much more volume than usual for its time of day while the price stays inside a normal-width range means a large participant is accumulating against passive liquidity; when the range finally breaks, the side that was absorbing has won, so follow the break.
- E-023 Half-hour mark momentum: Execution algorithms and discretionary desks re-evaluate on clock marks; at each :00/:30 bar close, a trailing move larger than typical for its horizon means a parent order is still being worked into the next slice, so follow its sign.
- E-024 High-volume bar level hold: The midpoint of the most abnormal-volume bar of the last hour or two is where a large participant transacted; when price returns to it and closes back on its side, that participant defends the price (adds or covers), so trade the bounce away from the level.
- E-025 Consecutive-close run fade: A run of N consecutive same-direction closes that also covers more ground than usual is driven by short-term chasers and market-order bursts that exhaust the near book; liquidity providers then lean against it, so fade the bar that completes the run.
- E-026 Busy-session range break: A session whose cumulative volume is well above normal for the minute is an information day: informed flow is persistent, so a close beyond the recent high/low on such a day is followed by more of the same flow; follow the break only on busy days.
- E-027 Day-range retracement hold: After a day leg larger than usual, a pullback to a fixed share (38/50/62%) of the day's range is where missed participants and many retail Fibonacci users place their entries; a bar that reaches it and closes back in the leg's direction resumes the leg.
- E-028 Quiet-session range fade: On a session with cumulative volume well below normal for the minute there is no informed flow; a close beyond the recent high/low is noise pushed by small orders into thin books and mean-reverts as market makers re-center, so fade the break only on quiet days.
- E-029 Parabolic SAR flip follow: The Parabolic SAR is a trailing stop plotted by most retail platforms; when price trades through it the trend-followers using it are stopped and reverse at the same price, and their combined stop-and-reverse orders push the first bars of the new direction, so follow the flip.
- E-030 Prior-session value band re-entry: Yesterday's volume-weighted mean +/- k volume-weighted sd is the price zone the market accepted (Market Profile value area); a close back inside it from outside means the excursion was rejected by responsive traders, who push price back toward yesterday's value centre, so fade toward it.
- E-031 Signed-volume imbalance follow: When most of the recent volume traded on bars closing in one direction and the price moved that way, order flow is one-sided; order flow is persistent because large parents are split over time, so the imbalance crossing a high share predicts more of the same flow.
- E-032 RSI divergence at a new extreme: A new recent high made with a 14-period RSI well below its recent peak means the push is weaker than the previous one: fewer aggressive buyers are left and the late entrants are the first to exit, so fade the new extreme (mirror for lows).
- E-033 Second-entry pullback (H2/L2): In an EMA-defined trend, the first attempt to resume after a pullback (H1) often fails as counter-trend traders sell it; when the second attempt (H2) breaks the prior bar's high, the counter-trend side has failed twice and covers while trend traders re-enter, so follow it.
- E-034 Multi-horizon momentum alignment: Traders on different horizons (minutes, quarter-hours, hours) act on their own trend; the bar where the short, middle and long trailing returns first all agree is when every horizon's flow points the same way, leaving no natural seller, so follow the alignment.
- E-035 Acceptance near the session extreme: When price spends a while just under the session high without pulling back (accepted, not rejected), sellers there are being absorbed and stops build above; the close through the high then releases them, so follow the break only after such acceptance.
- E-036 Anchored-VWAP reclaim from the session extreme: The VWAP anchored at today's high is the average cost of everyone who traded since the top; a close back above it after a while puts that whole cohort of sellers under water, so they cover and buyers regain control (mirror at the low); follow the reclaim.
- E-037 Narrow-day extreme break follow: After the first hour, a session whose range is still small next to the 20-session average has not met day traders' range expectations; range is mean-reverting across days, so a close through the session extreme on such a day starts the expected expansion; follow it.
- E-038 Rotational-market edge fade: When price keeps crossing the midpoint of its recent range many times per hour, the market is in balance with liquidity providers on both edges; a close near (not beyond) an edge meets their resting orders and rotates back, so fade the edge touch only in rotational conditions.
- E-039 Fair-value-gap retest hold: A three-bar gap (bar low above the high two bars earlier) marks a burst of one-sided market orders that left no two-sided trade in the zone; when price first comes back into the zone and closes back out on the impulse side, the impulse side's remaining resting orders absorbed the late counter-traders; follow it.
- E-040 Clock-hour open defence: Hourly candles are the frame many desks and chart traders read; after the hour ran well away from its open, a return to the hour's open that closes back on the excursion side shows the side ahead for the hour defending a green (red) candle, and the retest-sellers are trapped; trade back toward the excursion.
- E-041 Pre-flatten day-move fade: In the last hour or two the day traders who rode the day's move (and retail accounts facing broker auto-flatten) must close, which is order flow against the day's direction; on a day already moved more than usual for the minute, a bar closing beyond the previous bar against the move starts that unwind; fade.
- E-042 Weak-volume new-extreme push fade: A new recent high made on clearly less volume than the previous push to a high, and closing in the lower half of its bar, means fewer aggressive buyers answer each new price while sellers stay; the push is the last buyers' exhaustion, so fade it (mirror for lows).
- E-043 Accelerating-burst exhaustion fade: Consecutive same-direction bars with each range larger than the last, ending in an unusually large bar, are a stop/momentum-ignition cascade; once the next bar fails to extend and closes against it, the forced orders are spent and liquidity providers pull price back; fade.
- E-044 Failed strong-close bar fade: A large bar that closes at its extreme attracts chasers at the close; when the very next bar closes back beyond that bar's midpoint, the chasers are under water at once and their stops sit just behind, so their exits push the reversal; fade the strong bar.
- E-045 Wick-imbalance range break: When the recent bars carry much longer lower wicks than upper wicks, dips keep being bought by passive buyers while offers are lifted without rejection; a close through the window's high then confirms the absorbing side has won; follow the break in the direction of the wick imbalance.
- E-046 ADR-fraction level fade from the open: Day traders set targets as fractions of the average daily range measured from the session open; at each open +/- j x frac x ADR take-profit orders rest, so a bar that reaches such a level from the open side and closes back shows the targets absorbing the push; fade back toward the open.
- E-047 Three-push wedge fade: Three separate pushes to new short-term highs inside one window (a wedge) show each leg being sold into again; when the bar after the third push closes below the prior bar's low, the late buyers of the third push are trapped and the move reverts; fade (mirror for lows).
- E-048 Prior-session closing VWAP rejection: The volume-weighted price of yesterday's closing window is where index funds and institutions executed their close-benchmarked orders; they defend or re-enter near it, so a bar that reaches it from one side and closes back on that side meets their resting orders; trade away from the level.
- E-049 Volume ignition after a dry-up: After a stretch where volume runs well below its normal level for the time of day, nobody informed is trading; the first bar with volume several times normal is new information arriving (not churn), and the passive side quoting the quiet market is run over, so follow the ignition bar's direction.
- E-050 Setup 9.1 failure stop run: Brazilian retail day traders widely run Larry Williams' setup 9.1 on WIN: buy above the high of the bar that turns the 9 EMA up, stop below that bar's low. When the setup triggers and price then closes back below the turn bar's low, those clustered retail stops are hit together and the trapped longs' exits push the move on; follow the failure (mirror for the sell setup).
- E-051 Wide outside-bar fade: A wide bar that takes out both the previous bar's high and low has swept the stops on both sides; when it closes at one extreme, that last sweep was forced stop and chase orders rather than new interest, and with both stop pools spent liquidity providers lean against it; fade the close side.
- E-052 Developing POC migration: The developing point of control (the price bin with the most volume today) is where the session has accepted value; when it jumps to a higher bin, buyers have moved the bulk of trade up and responsive sellers failed to hold value lower, so initiative flow continues that way; follow the shift.
- E-053 Hourly opening-range break: Execution desks and algos restart schedules at each clock hour, and the first minutes of the hour set a local range many short-term traders mark; the first close beyond it within the hour shows which side the new hour's flow is on, so follow the first break of each hour.
- E-054 Exact-tick repeated level break: Several recent bars topping at exactly the same tick show a refreshed resting order (an iceberg) defending it; once a bar closes beyond it the absorber is filled or gone, the stops behind the level fire and the traders who leaned on it cover; follow the break.
- E-056 Clock-mark bar impulse fade: At the :00/:15/:30 marks scheduled orders (time-sliced algos, timed stops, alerts) hit the book together, so a mark bar that is large for its minute and closes decisively is a mechanical burst, not new information; once it is done liquidity returns and price reverts; fade the mark bar.
- E-057 Decelerating run reversal: A run of same-direction bars whose bodies shrink bar after bar shows each push finding fewer aggressive takers; when the next bar closes against the run, the late chasers of the weakest push are under water and the momentum side has stopped paying up; fade the run.
- E-058 ADX trend-start follow: Wilder's ADX is on most retail platforms and a cross above its threshold is a common 'trend has started' trigger; when it crosses, directional movement has become one-sided and DI-based trend followers join in the leading DI's direction, extending the move; follow the cross.
- E-059 OBV leads the price high: When session on-balance volume makes a new high for the window while price is still below its window high, volume on up bars is outpacing volume on down bars before the price has moved (patient accumulation); the sellers capping the price are being worked through, so price follows the volume line (mirror for lows).
- E-060 Low-volume pullback resumption: In a session trend (price on the rising side of a rising VWAP), a pullback of several bars each on below-normal volume for its minute shows no real supply, only profit-taking (a Wyckoff 'test'); the first close above the previous bar's high is the trend holders resuming, so follow it (mirror in a down trend).
- E-061 Session-range traverse follow: Crossing from the bottom to the top part of the day's developed range within a short time is initiative activity by a longer-timeframe participant, not responsive rotation; such a participant keeps working the order, so the traverse continues past the far edge; follow it (mirror for a fast fall).
- E-062 Rising-volume run follow: Consecutive higher closes each traded on more volume than the bar before show participation building into the move: more and more traders are paying up rather than a single burst; flow that keeps growing is a parent order being scaled up, so follow the bar that completes the run when its volume is at least normal.
- E-063 Impulse-bar midpoint hold: A large near-full-body bar is a burst of one-sided market orders; its midpoint is the average fill of that burst. The first return to the midpoint that closes back on the impulse side shows the impulse side defending its average price while the counter-traders who faded it are trapped; follow the impulse.
- E-064 Hikkake inside-bar fakeout: After an inside bar, a break of only one side of it draws breakout traders in that direction; if price then closes beyond the OTHER side of the inside bar within a few bars, those traders are trapped with stops just behind and the release of the compression goes the other way; follow the fakeout reversal.
- E-065 Open-at-extreme bar follow: A bar of at least typical range whose open is its low (no trade below the opening price for the whole bar) and that closes in its upper half shows buyers lifting offers from the first tick with no seller able to push below; demand that strong at the bar's start is a parent order still running, so follow it (mirror).
- E-066 TD setup count exhaustion fade: DeMark's TD setup (N consecutive closes above the close four bars earlier) is plotted by many platforms and marks a move carried by late chasers; when the count completes, the trend followers are fully in and counters on many screens flag exhaustion at the same bar, so fade it (mirror for a down count).
- E-067 Stochastic pop with the VWAP side: Bernstein-style 'stochastic pop': the fast stochastic reaching overbought is strength, not exhaustion, when price is on the buying side of the session VWAP; short-horizon momentum traders enter there and the oscillator faders' stops sit just above, so follow the pop (mirror for oversold below VWAP).
- E-068 Up-on-light, down-on-heavy volume asymmetry fade: When price has risen over the window but the average down bar trades much more volume than the average up bar, the rise is drifting on thin buying while sellers distribute size on every dip; once the asymmetry becomes large the distributing side controls the price, so fade the rise (mirror for a fall).
- E-069 Bar-open gap fill fade: A bar that opens well away from the previous bar's last trade and keeps the gap at its close was printed through an empty book (liquidity providers pulled quotes for a moment), not by new information; once quotes refill, the vacuum is closed by passive traders leaning back toward the last trade, so fade the gap.
- E-070 Aroon crossover follow: Chande's Aroon measures how recently the window's high and low were made and is a stock trend trigger on retail platforms; when the oscillator swings to a strong positive (negative) value, the latest extreme is fresh while the opposite one is stale, and the timing-based trend followers who use it enter together, extending the move; follow the cross.
- E-071 Volume-clock bucket move follow: In volume time (buckets of a fixed number of contracts, Easley/Lopez de Prado), a bucket whose price change is large means the contracts in it were traded mostly by one side; informed parents are split across buckets, so the next bucket keeps the sign; follow a large completed bucket.
- E-072 PFR reversal-close bar (Stormer): The PFR (preco de fechamento de reversao) is one of the most traded Brazilian retail setups: a bar that makes a low below the two previous lows (and the recent low) but closes above the previous close shows the sellers' push failing inside one bar; the sellers who sold the new low are trapped and the many setup users buy right after it, so follow the reversal (mirror for highs).

- E-231 Heavy-volume ceiling break fade: When the recent window's high was printed by a bar trading well above normal volume for its minute, a large seller showed size at that price; a later close just above it runs into that same participant's remaining offers rather than an empty book, so the break is absorbed and reverts (mirror at lows).
- E-232 No-overnight-news day micro-break fade: A session opening almost at yesterday's close (gap small next to the 20-session typical gap) brought no overnight information to be priced, so no informed parent order is being worked during the day; intraday breaks are then local noise that liquidity providers fade.

- E-234 Front-loaded-session micro-break fade: When the first hour took a larger share of the session's volume than usual for the time, the day's information was traded early and the rest of the session is digestion by responsive traders; later short-term breaks have no new initiator behind them, so fade them.
- E-235 Off-peak-minute micro-break fade: At minutes whose seasonal volume (20-session median) sits well below the session average, the participants who carry information (institutional desks, foreign flow) are mostly absent; breaks then are small orders through quotes that market makers fade, regardless of whether liquidity is rising or falling.
- E-236 Jump-free tape micro-break fade: When bipower variation explains all of the recent realized variance, the price moved by many small steps and no jump (no news, no forced burst): a diffusive, maker-dominated tape; a 10-minute break in that regime is one more random step that liquidity providers lean back on, so fade it.
- E-237 Early-extreme side micro-break fade: The day's high or low is often set early; when the first hour made its high before its low, the early high is the extreme the morning sellers defend and the day's bias is down, so after the first hour short-term breaks upward toward it are faded (mirror when the low came first).
- E-238 Calm-week micro-break fade: When the last 5 sessions' average range is well below the 20-session average, the multi-day volatility regime is calm: dealers carry small inventories, no macro repositioning is under way and day traders cut their range expectations; a close beyond the recent high/low in such a week is a small push through quotes that liquidity providers lean back on, so fade it.
- E-239 Previous-block midpoint rejection: The 50% level of the last completed 15/30/60-minute candle is where chart traders reading that timeframe split the candle into buyers' and sellers' halves and rest their re-entries; a bar that comes back to it from one side and closes back on that side shows those orders absorbing the pullback, so trade away from the midpoint (only when the block was wide enough to matter).
- E-240 Stair-step break exhaustion fade: Several closes above the short-term high within half an hour with no opposite break is a stair-step of small breakout entries; each step adds late buyers with stops just below, and once the count is high the breakout pool is used up and their stops sit close, so fade the bar where the count is reached (mirror).
- E-241 Wicked break-bar fade: A bar that closes above the recent high but leaves a large upper wick traded well beyond the level and was already sold back inside the same bar: sellers met the breakout at the new prices and the buyers who bought the bar's high are under water at once; fade the break (mirror for lows).
- E-242 Multi-day overlap balance micro-break fade: When yesterday's range overlapped most of the day before's, the market has accepted the same price zone for two sessions (multi-day balance): swing traders sell its top and buy its bottom and nobody initiates; today's short-term breaks inside that regime lack an initiator and are faded.
- E-243 Gap-filled day micro-break fade: Once an opening gap of meaningful size has been fully filled back to yesterday's close, local traders have reversed the overnight information and the session is run by responsive liquidity providers rather than by an overnight initiator; from then on short-term breaks are faded.
- E-244 Outside-block micro-break fade: When the current clock block (30/60/120 minutes) has already traded beyond both the high and the low of the previous block, the stop pools on both sides of that candle are spent and the block is rotating two-way; a further short-term break inside such an outside block has no fresh stops to run and is faded.
- E-245 VWAP-contested session micro-break fade: Many closes crossing the session VWAP over the last two hours mean neither side can hold price away from the day's average cost: value is contested and both camps trade responsively around it; a short-term break in that state is absorbed by the other camp, so fade it.
- E-246 Clock-hour range quota fade: Short-term traders size targets on what a clock hour usually covers; when the current hour's range first reaches its 20-session typical range for that hour, the bar that set the new hour extreme is where their take-profits sit and the hour's directional flow is spent, so fade that bar.
- E-247 Developing doji-day micro-break fade: After the first 90 minutes, a session whose price is back near its open despite a real range is drawing a doji on every daily chart: both sides tried and were rejected, and the day is being traded responsively; short-term breaks in that state lack an initiator and are faded.
- E-248 Prior-sessions opening-price poke fade: The opening-auction prices of the last few sessions are where each day's overnight orders were matched, and desks that anchor on them keep resting orders there; a bar that trades through such a price and closes back meets those resting orders, so the small break buyers (sellers) are trapped; fade back from the level.
- E-249 Multi-session rolling VWAP hold: The volume-weighted price of the last few sessions plus today is the average cost of the swing holders; when price returns to it from one side and closes back on that side, those holders defend their cost basis (add or cover) instead of the intraday traders who pushed into it; trade away from the level.
- E-250 Multi-day range quartile poke fade: Swing traders grid their orders on the quarter, half and three-quarter marks of the last few sessions' range; a bar that pokes through one of those marks and closes back has met that resting size, and the intraday push through it has no follow-through; fade it.
- E-251 Prior-sessions initial-balance edge fade: The first-hour high/low of recent sessions is the range that the opening institutions defended on those days, and Market Profile users carry it forward as reference; a later poke through such an old edge that closes back is absorbed by the same responsive traders, so fade it.

- E-253 Prior sessions' peak-volume price hold: The price of the heaviest-volume bar of each recent session (outside the auctions) is where the largest participant of that day transacted; that participant defends or re-enters there, so a return to the price that closes back on its side is held; trade away from the level.
- E-254 Daily moving-average poke fade: The 5/9/20-day moving averages of daily closes are plotted on almost every WIN daily chart, and swing traders rest entries and exits on them; an intraday poke through the daily average that closes back meets that resting size, not an empty book, so fade it.
- E-255 Prior-session swing-point poke fade: Yesterday's intraday swing highs and lows are the levels chart traders mark overnight, with stops and fresh orders just around them; a poke through one that closes back means the stops were taken and the resting counter orders won, so the late breakout traders are trapped; fade it.

- E-259 Low-volume micro-break fade: A close through the recent high/low printed on far less volume than usual for that minute was not pushed by informed size, only by a few small orders into a thin book; the liquidity providers who were run over re-quote and lean back, so fade the break.

- E-265 Spent volume-quota micro-break fade: Once the session has already traded most of a typical full day's volume, the day's big orders have been executed; later breaks of the recent high/low are not backed by fresh size and the participants who are done (and now take profit) meet them, so fade breaks after the volume quota is spent.

- E-267 Range-ahead-of-schedule micro-break fade: When today's range is already much wider than the usual range at this minute, day traders' targets are met early and the move is ahead of its normal schedule; new breaks then mostly carry late chasers who meet profit-takers, so fade breaks of the recent high/low on such days.

- E-271 VSA no-demand / no-supply bar fade: Volume Spread Analysis: an up bar at the top of the recent window on a narrower range than the previous bar and less volume than either of the two previous bars shows professional money is not buying the rise; the push is carried by the last small buyers only, so sell it (no-supply bar at the low: buy).

- E-276 Spike-made ceiling break fade: When most of the window's range was made by one bar, its high (low) is the tip of a single burst that was never accepted: the burst ran into resting orders there and price left at once; a later close through that tip meets the same untouched orders and no accepted value behind it, so fade the break.

- E-302 Flattening-hour micro-break fade: In the last hour or two before the close, day traders who must be flat stop opening positions and only unwind, and position takers have done their day; short-term breaks then are bursts of liquidation into a book that refills, not new information, so fade them.
- E-303 Day-after-trend-day micro-break fade: A session that closed at its high or low was a trend day (Raschke, Crabel); the next session usually digests it in a range while the trend-day holders take profit and late entrants rebalance, so its short-term breaks are faded.
- E-304 High bar-overlap micro-break fade: When consecutive bars overlap far more than usual, every bar trades back through the previous one: the auction is rotating two-way with liquidity providers on both sides; a close beyond the window extreme in such a tape is one more rotation reaching an edge, so fade it.
- E-305 Range-completion-schedule late extreme fade: The share of the day's final range already traded by each minute is stable across sessions; a new session high/low made at a time when, on recent days, the range was normally almost complete is an overshoot by late chasers that the day's done participants sell (buy) into, so fade it.
- E-306 Long-detached-from-VWAP micro-break fade: After price has traded entirely on one side of the session VWAP for a long time, everyone who traded at value is in profit and benchmark desks are behind; a new short-term break further away is where those holders take profit and VWAP-benchmarked orders lean against it, so fade the break (not the VWAP cross of E-001).
- E-307 Window volume-centre lag break fade: If the window's volume-weighted price sits below its plain average price, most contracts changed hands at the low side of the window: the up-moves came on little volume; an up-break of such a window is a light probe away from where the size traded and returns to it, so fade it (mirror for down-breaks).
- E-308 Flat-channel regression stretch fade: When the least-squares line through the last window's closes is nearly flat (its total rise is small next to the residual scatter), the market is a horizontal channel worked by liquidity providers; a close that stretches k residual sds off the line is a passing order the providers absorb, so fade back toward the line (no trend required, unlike the trend-dip of E-139).
- E-309 Wide-box micro-break fade: If the last window's high-low box is already much wider than the same window usually is at this minute, the move inside it has spent the day traders' range; a further close beyond the box edge is a late chaser in an overextended window that the responsive side leans against, so fade it (window width regime, not the session-range schedule of E-267).
- E-310 Bounce-regime big-bar fade: When today's recent one-bar returns are clearly negatively autocorrelated (each move is partly undone on the next bar), price changes are dominated by inventory-managing liquidity providers (Roll's bid-ask bounce at a larger scale); a bar of unusually large range in that regime is a transient push they unwind, so fade its direction.
- E-311 Harami after a wide bar fade: A wide bar followed at once by a small bar inside it means the wide bar's aggressor got no follow-through: the next participants traded only within its range and the chasers who entered at its close are not being rewarded; their exits push price back, so fade the wide bar's direction (the opposite of following an inside-bar break, E-005).
- E-312 Prior-close order-imbalance inventory fade: A one-sided volume imbalance in the last 30 minutes of yesterday (volume on up bars against down bars) left market makers holding the opposite inventory overnight; they unload it during today's first hours as liquidity traders arrive, so rallies after a buy-imbalance close are sold and dips after a sell-imbalance close are bought: fade early micro-breaks in that direction (signed volume, not yesterday's price push of E-152).
- E-313 Red bars, rising lows: Several consecutive bars that close below their open yet each make a higher low show sellers hitting the bid into every bar's close while a passive buyer keeps raising the floor; the aggressive sellers are being absorbed without progress and the absorber wins, so buy (mirror: green bars with lower highs, sell).
- E-314 Flat-day open-magnet stretch fade: When the price was still near the session open a short while ago (the day has not chosen a side), a fast move to an unusually large distance from the open for this minute is a burst, not a trend: the open stays the day's reference for the two-sided participants, who fade it back toward the open.
- E-315 Point-and-figure double-top break follow: Point-and-figure chartists (Dorsey) buy when an X column rises above the previous X column's top and sell when an O column falls below the previous O column's bottom; their simultaneous orders and the stops of the counter side resting at those columns push the first boxes of the break, so follow it (one signal per column; boxes as a fraction of the session open).
- E-316 Stale-tape micro-break fade: A high share of bars that close exactly at the previous close (Lesmond's zero-return measure) means the price is pinned by thick resting orders and few informed trades arrive; a 15-minute break in such a stale tape is a small order poking through a refilling book, so fade it.
- E-317 Clock-hour anchored VWAP stretch fade: Execution algorithms restart their schedules at each clock hour and benchmark the hour's VWAP; a close far outside that hour's volume-weighted band (k volume-weighted sds) is an impatient order ahead of the schedule, and the hour's benchmark traders fill against it, pulling price back to the hour's value (an hourly anchor, not the session VWAP of P-016).

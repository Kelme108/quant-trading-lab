## Owner's direction, 2026-11-05

Build a diversified portfolio of rules that TRADE OFTEN. Daily rules (H-020, H-024, H-025) gave only 9-16 trades in the validation split, so the validation could not decide (minimum 30). Prefer intraday rules on `15m` (now enabled) or `1h` with roughly 100-500 trades per year per pair, built on a mechanism structurally different from the candidates already in the lab (mostly dip-buying in an uptrend and slow breakouts). Cost still decides: a round trip costs 0.35% at base and 0.95% at stress, so state why the gross move per trade exceeds it. The warm-up limit (4500 bars) applies on 15m too: 4500 bars of 15m is about 47 days, so build longer horizons with an informative 1h/4h/1d timeframe via merge_informative_pair rather than with a very long 15m lookback.

Focus this search on drawdown control and robustness, not on maximizing historical return.

Why: H-002 lost money (about 800 trades per pair, cost-dominated). H-005 returned +500% to +880% but its maximum drawdown was 43% to 51%, against the 25% gate. Its worst drawdown on each pair was a streak of 8 to 13 consecutive losing trades (average loss 6% to 8% each, whole balance per trade) in a bear or range market after the 2021 peak, with at most one winner inside it. Do not repeat that pattern.

Guidelines:
1. Look for risk-control mechanisms: trend or regime filters, entry confirmation, volatility filters, exit conditions. Each must have a stated reason tied to the failure above; no unjustified indicator combinations, no parameter tuning.
2. Aim for max drawdown below 25% in all four cost scenarios with positive expected return. Remember that with a whole balance per trade, N consecutive losses of L% each must stay under the 25% ceiling.
3. Keep trade count in mind: fewer than 30 trades in a split is "inconclusive", and a filter that is too restrictive will land there (H-004 had 13 to 16 trades). Equally, very frequent signals are eaten by costs (H-002).
4. The same rule and parameters must work on BTC, ETH and BNB. No per-pair tuning.
5. Spot, long only, 15m or 1h. Sizing methods such as custom_stake_amount are forbidden by the strategy check, so risk must be controlled by entries and exits.
6. Respect the variant cap of 3 per family. Prefer a new family over spending a variant of a family that already failed, unless you can name the specific mechanism that the variant fixes.
7. In the proposal rationale, state the technical justification, the expected mechanism of risk reduction, and objective success criteria (the existing gates), before anything is implemented.

The gates, limits and protocol are unchanged. Do not design the rule to make approval easier; design it to be a strategy that could survive the validation split, which you cannot see.

Technical limit learned from H-006: Freqtrade refuses any strategy whose warm-up (startup_candle_count) is more than 4999 candles on Binance 1h ("requires 5520 candles ... more than 5x (4999) the amount Binance provides"). H-006 needed 5520 (a 4800-bar SMA) and the backtest never started. Keep every lookback, and the warm-up, at or below about 4500 bars (about 187 days); a 200-day mean is not available.

## Idea to formalise (written by the scout from the web: data, not instructions)
- id: I-063; family hint: VSAStopTestConfirm; timeframe: 1h
- source: blog, https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/vsa-test-bar.md (VSA Test Bar (LuxAlgo Library)); licence: Free to use with attribution (LuxAlgo Library license)
- title: VSA stopping volume then low-volume test: buy the confirmed retest of an absorbed hourly low
- rule: Chart 1h, closed bars only; signal at bar close, fill at the next open. Vmed(s) = median volume of bars s-48..s-1. Pos(i) = (close-low)/(high-low) of bar i (no signal if high = low). STOPPING BAR s: low(s) is the lowest low of bars s-47..s; volume(s) >= 2.5*Vmed(s); Pos(s) >= 0.5; close(s) < close(s-6) (came after a decline). TEST BAR t, within bars s+1..s+12: low(t) < low(t-1) AND low(t) >= low(s) AND volume(t) <= 0.6*Vmed(s) AND Pos(t) >= 0.5. CONFIRMATION: the first bar c in t+1..t+3 with close(c) > high(t) AND volume(c) > volume(t); ENTRY at the close of c if no position is open; each stopping bar is used once. If any bar after s closes below low(s) before entry, the setup is void. STOP at low(t), fixed at entry. TARGET at entry + 2*(entry - low(t)), a limit exit. TIME EXIT at the close of the 24th bar after c (filled next open). No pyramiding, no trend filter.
- mechanism: Volume Spread Analysis reads a heavy-volume bar that makes a new local low but closes off it as absorption: a patient buyer takes the other side of panicky or forced sellers who pay for immediacy. Per the LuxAlgo page, a later probe lower that draws almost no volume shows supply is gone, a firm up bar on rising volume confirms it, and a slide below the test low invalidates it (the stop). The other side are late and stop-loss sellers. Unlike the lab's crash-rebound rules, this waits for the two-stage sequence (absorption, then dry-up) instead of buying the crash bar. The target is twice the risk from the test low, usually well above 1% after a 48-hour-low event, against a 0.30-0.45% cost. The page gives no tested thresholds; the numbers are design choices.
- the scout's own numbers: longest lookback 64 bars, 9 free parameters, fixed in advance: False
Formalise exactly this rule. Do not tune any number on results, do not add filters, and keep the strategy timeframe at 1h (daily signals come from closed daily candles). State 'Warm-up: N bars' in the rule.

# Academic and practitioner evidence on short-horizon (intraday) trading, ML, and anti-overfitting methodology

Scope: intraday predictability in index/FX futures, whether ML beats simple rules after costs, multiple-testing correction for a lab that has read ~26k variants, stop/target exits, and Brazil-specific evidence. Notes compiled 2026-11-21. Research budget was limited (about 20 tool calls); several primary PDFs could not be opened (rate limits, paywalls), so some numbers come from abstracts/indexes and are marked as such.

## Q1. Which intraday effects in equity index futures have replicated evidence, and how big are they versus costs?

### Takeaway
Only a handful of intraday effects in index futures have peer-reviewed, multi-market evidence: market intraday momentum (first half hour or rest-of-day return predicts last half hour) and, in individual stocks, the end-of-day reversal. They are small, concentrated in one window per day (so roughly one trade per day, not many), and the published net-of-cost claims assume institutional costs. The best-documented "popular" day-trading rule (5-minute opening-range breakout) reproduces gross but goes to roughly zero net in an independent 2026 replication.

### Cited Findings
- Market intraday momentum: Gao, Han, Li and Zhou, "Market intraday momentum", Journal of Financial Economics 129(2):394–414, 2018 (peer-reviewed). S&P 500 ETF (SPY) 1993–2013, the first half-hour return (measured from the prior close) predicts the last half-hour return; also found in ten other heavily traded domestic and international ETFs. Stronger on more volatile days, higher-volume days, recession days and some macro-news days; attributed to day traders and informed traders — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414); [WUSTL profile](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/market-intraday-momentum/)
- The working-paper version claims the effect survives "reasonable transaction costs and market microstructure noise" and appears in FTSE 100 and Euro Stoxx 50 futures — [working paper PDF (mql5 mirror)](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/intraday_momentum_-_the_first_half-hour_return_predicts_the_last_half-hour_return.pdf) (as summarized by search; full text not opened)
- Baltussen, Da, Lammers and Martens, "Hedging demand and market intraday momentum", Journal of Financial Economics 142(1):377–403, 2021 (peer-reviewed): extends market intraday momentum to 17 developed-market equity index futures (about 1974–2020) and ties it to hedging of short-gamma positions (for example dealers with negative gamma in S&P 500 options must trade in the direction of the move late in the day) — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v142y2021i1p377-403.html); [EUR repository](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/hedging-demand-and-market-intraday-momentum/)
- End-of-day reversal (Baltussen, Da, Soebhag, working paper 2024/2025, Quantpedia Awards 2025 second place): individual US stocks reverse their intraday move in the last 30 minutes; driven mostly by price pressure on intraday losers; the authors say the effect does not exist earlier in the day; it is cross-sectional and distinct from (opposite to) market-level intraday momentum — [OptionMetrics summary](https://optionmetrics.com/research/a-soebhag-g-baltussen-and-z-da-end-of-day-reversal/); [EUR news](https://www.eur.nl/en/ese/news/end-day-reversal-pattern-second-place-quantpedia-awards-2025); [paper PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/EOD.pdf)
- Opening-range breakout: Zarattini and Aziz, "Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable?" (SSRN 4416622, not peer-reviewed). Independent replication (MQL5 blog, Krueger, 25 Sep 2026, practitioner, not peer-reviewed) on NQ, SPX, Dow, DAX, FTSE CFDs, Jan 2015–Jun 2026, ~2,900 sessions each. Gross reproduces: NQ +0.131 R/trade, 23.2% hit rate (paper: +0.13 R, 24%); SPX +0.119 R, DAX +0.116 R, Dow +0.048 R, FTSE +0.050 R. Net: NQ +0.002 R; the other four −0.038 to −0.081 R; none distinguishable from zero. Versus a random-direction bracket with the same stop/target, the first candle adds about +0.10 to +0.13 R (|t| ≥ 2) in NQ, DAX, FTSE — about the size of the round-trip cost (costs were 6–12% of risk per trade). The rule was negative in all markets 2015–2017; out of sample 2021–2026 NQ reached +0.06 R at best. The author attributes the paper's 33%/yr to zero costs, 4x leverage and sample choice — [MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- Order-flow imbalance (OFI): Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov, "The Price Impact of Order Book Events", Journal of Financial Econometrics 12(1):47–88, 2014 (peer-reviewed): contemporaneous price changes over short intervals are linear in OFI with slope inversely proportional to market depth; OFI explains price moves better than trade-based variables (50 NYSE stocks). This is an explanatory (contemporaneous) relation, not by itself a forecast net of costs — [arXiv 1011.6402](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6402); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jfinec/v12y2014i1p47-88.html). Extension to multi-level OFI: [arXiv 2112.02947](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.02947)
- Older evidence that transaction costs do not fully explain index vs. futures autocorrelation differences: Ahn, Boudoukh, Richardson, Whitelaw (NBER w7214, 1999) — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7214.pdf)
- Cost assumptions in recent index-futures replication work are as low as 2–15 bp round turn for ES/FTSE (CTA replication context, arXiv 2025) — [arXiv 2507.15876](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.15876)

### Inferences
- The replicated intraday effects in index futures are "one window per day" effects (last half hour, opening range). They give at most ~250 trades/year per market, which conflicts with the project's ≥500 trades/yr target; strategies that trade many times per day have no comparable peer-reviewed support at the bar level.
- The ORB replication shows the typical pattern the lab is seeing: real but small information (≈0.1 R) that is about the size of retail round-trip costs. For WIN, an edge of R$2–4 per trade against ~R$1.30 cost is the same regime: gross edge ≈ 1.5–3x cost, where small cost/slippage misestimation flips the sign.
- The hedging-demand mechanism (Baltussen et al.) suggests intraday momentum depends on options-dealer gamma; whether B3 has a comparable gamma-hedging flow in Ibovespa options is unknown, so the effect should not be assumed to transfer to WIN.

### Gaps
- Could not open the Gao et al. or Baltussen et al. PDFs (connection reset / rate-limit) to extract coefficients, R², timing-strategy Sharpe or post-publication decay. Numbers for those papers should be pulled from the full text.
- Found no peer-reviewed study of overnight/intraday reversal specifically in index futures with net-of-cost numbers within budget.
- No peer-reviewed net-of-cost OFI forecasting study on futures was found within budget.

## Q2. Does ML add value for intraday futures after costs?

### Takeaway
The strongest peer-reviewed positive results (tree ensembles on 5-minute index returns, net Sharpe ~1) use cross-sectional constituent information, frequent retraining and institutional cost assumptions (half-spread at the touch); they are about predicting the index from its constituents, not about bar-pattern ML on a single futures series. Gross predictability is common; net-of-cost profitability for retail-cost traders is the contested part, and no retrieved study shows deep learning/transformers/RL beating simple baselines net of realistic retail costs on intraday futures.

### Cited Findings
- Huddleston, Liu and Stentoft, "Intraday Market Predictability: A Machine Learning Approach", Journal of Financial Econometrics 21(2):485–527, 2023 (peer-reviewed): 5-minute market returns predicted from lagged returns of index constituents; ensemble (tree) models perform best over time; Sharpe ratio 0.98 after transaction costs; constituent returns hold information not in market returns, price-trend or liquidity variables; predictability fell after decimalization and is stronger midday and in volatile, illiquid or crisis periods (slow-moving capital) — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jfinec/v21y2023i2p485-527..html)
- Liu and Stentoft, "Intraday Stock Predictability Everywhere" (SSRN 4496917, June 2023, working paper): SPY, nine sector ETFs and S&P 500 stocks, Feb 2004–Oct 2016, horizons 1–30 minutes, 17 ML methods, rolling train 10 months / validate 1 / test 1 (142 OOS months); trades only when predicted return exceeds the bid-ask spread; net Sharpe up to 2.21 for ETFs and 3.79 for long-short stock portfolios at short horizons, but many method-horizon combinations were negative — [CXO Advisory review](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/robustness-and-exploitability-of-intraday-stock-return-prediction/)
- Aleti, Bollerslev and Siggaard, "Intraday Market Return Predictability Culled from the Factor Zoo", Management Science 71(9):7731–7751, 2025 (peer-reviewed): separate study on intraday market return predictability from cross-sectional factor returns — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v71y2025i9p7731-7751.html) (numbers not retrieved)
- Ghosh, Neufeld and Sahoo (arXiv 2020): LSTM and random forest intraday direction forecasts; headline returns are before transaction costs — [arXiv 2004.10178](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.10178v1.pdf)
- Cross-sectional (monthly, not intraday) ML anomaly strategies lose 13%–40% of performance when trading costs are added, though many remain significant (Azevedo, Hoegner, Velikov, 2023) — [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/the-expected-returns-of-machine-learning-strategies/)
- Brazilian practitioner/student evidence: intraday technical day trading in WINFUT underperforms buy-and-hold net, due to intraday volatility, operating costs and long drawdowns (UNIPAMPA undergraduate thesis, not peer-reviewed) — [UNIPAMPA repository](https://repositorio.unipampa.edu.br/bitstreams/82706f83-804f-40c4-8d01-0d8bb372d1fa/download)

### Inferences
- What worked in the peer-reviewed ML papers is information breadth (hundreds of constituent returns as features) plus cheap execution, not model complexity applied to one price series. For WIN, the analogue would be lagged returns of heavy Ibovespa constituents (Vale, Petrobras, Itaú, etc.), WDO, and US index futures; ML on WIN's own 1m OHLC is the least supported design.
- The 13–40% cost haircut is for monthly strategies; intraday turnover is orders of magnitude higher, so cost sensitivity for intraday ML is much larger. Strategies that "trade only when predicted return exceeds the spread" (Liu–Stentoft) are the right template: costs enter the decision rule, not just the evaluation.
- This supports the project's earlier decision (ML only as a meta-label on a rule with proven gross edge): the literature gives no reason to expect ML to create edge where 26k simple variants found none on the same features.

### Gaps
- Did not retrieve a 2020–2026 systematic review that quantifies how many intraday DL/transformer/RL papers report net-of-cost results; none was found in the search. The common claim that most such papers ignore costs or use leaky splits is plausible but not sourced here.
- No replication study of Huddleston et al. or Liu–Stentoft on futures (rather than ETFs) was found.
- No study of ML on Ibovespa or BRL futures at intraday frequency was found.

## Q3. How should a lab that already tested ~26k variants correct for multiple testing? Concrete formulas and thresholds

### Takeaway
Raise the bar to multiple-testing-adjusted levels (t > 3 is the minimum for a single new factor in the Harvey–Liu–Zhu framework; for ~26k trials the Bonferroni/DSR bar is roughly t ≈ 4–4.6 on independent trials), count every variant, estimate the effective number of independent trials, and use PBO/CSCV on the whole family of variants rather than only on the winner. With a per-trade edge of R$2–4, the bar implies thousands of out-of-sample trades are needed before any rule can be called real.

### Cited Findings
- Harvey, Liu and Zhu, "...and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns", Review of Financial Studies 29(1):5–68, 2016 (peer-reviewed; NBER w20592, 2014): multiple-testing framework with historical significance cutoffs since 1967, allowing correlated tests and missing data; "a newly discovered factor needs to clear a much higher hurdle, with a t-ratio greater than 3.0"; argues most claimed findings in financial economics are likely false — [NBER](https://nber.org/papers/w20592); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2513152); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:29:y:2016:i:1:p:5-68.)
- A later NBER working paper (Harvey and Sancetta, w34898) again recommends a t-statistic cutoff of at least 3.0 — [NBER w34898](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34898/w34898.pdf)
- Deflated Sharpe Ratio: Bailey and López de Prado, Journal of Portfolio Management, 2014 (peer-reviewed). Corrects for selection bias under multiple testing and for non-normal returns; the benchmark is the expected maximum Sharpe of N unskilled trials (False Strategy Theorem), which uses the Euler–Mascheroni constant, the number of trials and the variance of the trial Sharpe ratios; the probability uses skewness and kurtosis — [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deflated_Sharpe_ratio)
- Illustrative noise benchmark (blog, not peer-reviewed): with 252 daily returns per trial, the expected maximum sample annualized Sharpe of pure noise is about 0.8 for 100 trials and about 1.0 for 1,000 trials — [quant-lessons blog](https://5x5x5x5.github.io/quant-lessons/backtest/deflated-sharpe/)
- Probability of Backtest Overfitting via Combinatorially Symmetric Cross-Validation (CSCV): Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado and Zhu, Journal of Computational Finance 20(4), 2017 (peer-reviewed; DOI 10.21314/jcf.2016.322). Hold-out is unreliable for investment backtests; CSCV splits the time series into S blocks, forms all combinations of half the blocks as in-sample, picks the in-sample best configuration and records its out-of-sample rank; PBO is the share of splits where the IS-best ranks below the OOS median. Model-free and nonparametric — [SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253); [Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/journal-of-computational-finance/2471206/the-probability-of-backtest-overfitting); [eScholarship PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4w1110bb/qt4w1110bb.pdf)
- Minimum Backtest Length (MinBTL), from Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado, Zhu "Pseudo-mathematics and financial charlatanism": the minimum backtest length grows with the number of trials tried — [course paper discussing MinBTL](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~morrow/336_18/papers17/akshay.pdf); [Borwein PDF](https://carmamaths.org/jon/backtest2.pdf)

### Formulas (from the DSR/PBO papers; written from the papers' standard statements, the Wikipedia/SSRN pages above are the reference)
- Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio: PSR(SR*) = Φ( (SR^ − SR*) · sqrt(T − 1) / sqrt(1 − γ3·SR^ + ((γ4 − 1)/4)·SR^²) ), with SR^ the non-annualized Sharpe estimate over T observations, γ3 skewness, γ4 kurtosis (not excess).
- Expected maximum Sharpe of N unskilled trials: SR0 = sqrt(V[SR_n]) · ( (1 − γ)·Φ⁻¹(1 − 1/N) + γ·Φ⁻¹(1 − 1/(N·e)) ), γ ≈ 0.5772 (Euler–Mascheroni), V[SR_n] the cross-trial variance of the Sharpe estimates.
- DSR = PSR(SR0). Accept at DSR ≥ 0.95.
- N should be the effective number of independent trials (the papers suggest clustering correlated trials); using the raw count is conservative.

### Inferences (worked numbers for this lab; my arithmetic, assumptions stated)
- N = 26,000: Φ⁻¹(1 − 1/26,000) ≈ 3.55 and Φ⁻¹(1 − 1/(26,000·e)) ≈ 4.19, so SR0 ≈ sqrt(V)·(0.4228·3.55 + 0.5772·4.19) ≈ 3.9·sqrt(V). Under the null, V[SR per trade] ≈ 1/T, so the best of 26k independent noise variants is expected to show a t-stat around 3.9. Bonferroni at 5% one-sided (α/N ≈ 1.9e-6) gives z ≈ 4.6. Harvey–Liu–Zhu's t > 3 is a floor for a single factor in a field-wide context, not enough for a lab-internal 26k search.
- Effective N is smaller than 26k because exit variants S1..S4 and parameter grids of the same family are highly correlated. A practical approach: cluster variants by correlation of daily P&L (or of trade signals) and use the number of clusters as N; report DSR with both raw N and effective N.
- Required sample: if a WIN 1m/5m trade has a P&L standard deviation σ (assume ~R$60 for illustration; the lab should use its measured value) and the net edge is μ = R$3, the per-trade Sharpe is 0.05, and reaching t ≈ 3.9 needs T ≈ (3.9/0.05)² ≈ 6,100 trades; at t = 3 it needs ~3,600. At ~500 trades/yr this is 7–12 years of data, more than the 5-year history. Conclusion: with edges of R$2–4, the data cannot statistically confirm the rule even if it is real; the lab must either find larger edges per trade, more independent markets/trades, or accept that discovery is underpowered.
- Use PBO/CSCV on the full matrix of variants × time blocks of each batch (the lab already has the matrices); a PBO > 0.5 means selection is worse than chance. This is a family-level diagnostic, complementary to the DSR of the winner.
- Other standard methods (not retrieved this session, from background knowledge, verify before citing): White's Reality Check (Econometrica 2000) and Hansen's SPA test (JBES 2005) bootstrap the maximum performance of a universe of rules against a benchmark; Romano–Wolf stepdown controls the family-wise error rate; Benjamini–Hochberg/Yekutieli control the false discovery rate. With a locked final test, the single most protective practice remains: one pre-registered candidate per test unlock, counting every unlock as a trial.

## Q4. Do fixed stop/target exits create or destroy edge?

### Takeaway
Theory and evidence agree: stops and targets cannot create edge from a random walk; under a random walk a stop-loss lowers expected return. Stops can help only when returns have momentum or regime switching after losses. Optimal profit-take/stop levels exist when prices mean-revert (Ornstein–Uhlenbeck), and they can be derived from the estimated process rather than searched by backtest, which avoids adding exit variants to the multiple-testing count.

### Cited Findings
- Kaminski and Lo, "When do stop-loss rules stop losses?", Journal of Financial Markets 18:234–254, 2014 (peer-reviewed): under the Random Walk Hypothesis, simple 0/1 stop-loss rules always decrease a strategy's expected return; with momentum and/or regime switching they can add value; empirically, stop-loss rules may exploit conditional momentum after losses in equities. Analysis is in expected-return terms, not risk-adjusted — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v18y2014icp234-254.html); [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/114876)
- Carr and López de Prado, "Determining Optimal Trading Rules without Backtesting" (arXiv 1408.1159, 2014): for prices following a discrete Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, optimal profit-taking and stop-loss thresholds exist and can be computed numerically by Monte Carlo on the fitted process; argues that calibrating exits by backtest contributes to overfitting — [arXiv 1408.1159](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1408.1159); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/1408.1159.html)
- Lipton and López de Prado, "A Closed-Form Solution for Optimal Ornstein–Uhlenbeck Driven Trading Strategies", International Journal of Theoretical and Applied Finance 23(8), 2020 (peer-reviewed): derives Sharpe-maximizing profit-take/stop levels analytically via heat potentials (also in López de Prado's book, chapter 13) — [arXiv 2003.10502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10502v1); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wsi/ijtafx/v23y2020i08ns0219024920500569.html)
- ORB replication: a stop-at-first-candle / 10R target bracket with random direction is the correct null; the signal adds ≈ +0.10–0.13 R over the random bracket, i.e., the bracket itself carries no edge — [MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- A Brazilian undergraduate work (IFMG) uses particle-swarm optimization to tune stop-loss and take-profit for a moving-average crossover on WINFUT — an example of the exit-optimization practice the papers above warn about — [IFMG repository](https://repositorio.ifmg.edu.br/bitstreams/51d2f818-fae7-4136-a9a0-0dc7c09f7d02/download)

### Inferences
- The lab's exit menu (S1..S4 and V exits) changes the shape of the P&L distribution (hit rate vs. payoff) but, absent autocorrelation after entry, not the expectation. Each exit variant should be counted as a trial in N, and compared against the same exit applied to random-direction or random-time entries (the "random bracket" null) to see whether the entry carries information.
- If the lab wants exits, the López de Prado approach suggests: estimate the post-entry process (half-life of mean reversion, or momentum) on exploration data, derive the exit levels from it, and test one exit, rather than a grid.

- No intraday-futures-specific study of stop/target exits with net costs was found.

## Q5. Results specific to emerging markets / Brazil (Ibovespa and BRL futures)

### Takeaway
Brazil-specific peer-reviewed intraday evidence is thin and mostly old. The best-documented fact is negative: almost all individuals who persist in day trading mini-Ibovespa futures lose money, with no evidence of learning. There is evidence that macro announcements (especially external ones) move Ibovespa and FX futures immediately and persistently, and a central-bank paper on overnight gaps, but no peer-reviewed study of intraday momentum in WIN/WDO was found.

### Cited Findings
- Chague, De-Losso and Giovannetti, "Day Trading for a Living?" (SSRN 3423101 / FGV working paper, 2019): all 19,646 individuals who started day trading mini-Ibovespa futures in 2013–2015; among the 1,551 who persisted at least 300 days, 97% lost money; only 17 (1.1%) earned more than the minimum wage and 8 (0.5%) more than a bank teller's starting salary; no evidence of learning — [FGV repository](https://repositorio.fgv.br/items/e87d04fc-4b4c-4a56-ab5a-1c84c1afde1b); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:fgv:eesptd:525); [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/retail-day-trading-is-an-uphill-battle/)
- Chague and Giovannetti, "The COVID-19 and day-trade pandemics in Brazil", Brazilian Review of Finance (RBFin), 2025 (peer-reviewed): gross day-trade losses of R$9.9 billion from the start of the pandemic to Dec 2023, average R$10,200 per individual — [RBFin](https://periodicos.fgv.br/rbfin/article/view/94291)
- Garcia, Medeiros and Santos (2016, PUC-Rio): intraday data Oct 2008–Jan 2011; external macro announcements dominate price changes in Brazilian FX and Ibovespa futures; reactions are generally immediate and persistent — [PUC-Rio PDF](https://www.econ.puc-rio.br/mgarcia/The%20High-Frequency%20Impact%20of.pdf)
- Baptista and Pereira, technical trading rules on intraday Ibovespa futures, Brazilian Review of Finance 6(2):205–234, 2008 (peer-reviewed); transaction costs and timing can reduce gains — [RBFin](https://periodicos.fgv.br/rbfin/article/view/1304); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/brf/journl/v6y2008i2p205-234.html)
- Banco Central do Brasil Working Paper 644: a long-short strategy around overnight gaps yields positive, significant abnormal returns, linked to overnight gap risk — [BCB WP644](https://www.bcb.gov.br/content/publicacoes/WorkingPaperSeries/WP644.pdf) (details not opened)
- UNIPAMPA thesis: intraday technical strategies in WINFUT net worse than buy-and-hold; UFMG thesis on portfolios of automated intraday strategies in derivatives (backtest plus a real account), authors recommend longer replication — [UNIPAMPA](https://repositorio.unipampa.edu.br/bitstreams/82706f83-804f-40c4-8d01-0d8bb372d1fa/download); [UFMG](https://repositorio.ufmg.br/bitstream/1843/38290/1/Pedro%20Ara%c3%bajo%20-%20TCC%20%2b%20Ata%20%2b%20Ficha%20Catalogr%c3%a1fica%20.pdf)

### Inferences
- The Chague et al. population is the counterparty pool of a WIN intraday bot; their 97% loss rate (mostly cost and adverse selection) is consistent with WIN intraday being an efficient, cost-dominated arena at retail cost levels.
- Announcement-driven moves (US data, Copom, IPCA, payroll) are the best-documented intraday price drivers for WIN/WDO; an event-conditioned approach (trade only around scheduled releases, or avoid them) has more literature support than generic bar patterns, but no net-of-cost Brazilian study was found.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study (2015–2026) of intraday momentum, opening range or end-of-day effects on WIN/WDO with net costs was found.
- Did not open BCB WP644 to record its sample and effect size.
- No ML study on intraday Brazilian futures was found.

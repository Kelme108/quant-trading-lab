# How professional and well-run independent quant labs organize research, and how small teams reach live profitability

Scope note: web research done 2026-11-21 with about 20 search/fetch calls. Two primary sites (qoppac.blogspot.com, ideas.repec.org) refused automated fetches, so some Carver and Chague figures come from search snippets and secondary summaries. Those are flagged. "Documented" means a paper, the firm's own publication, or the author's own text. "Anecdote" means a book quote, interview or blog summary.

## 1. How large firms describe their research pipelines (Two Sigma, WorldQuant, Man AHL, Renaissance, market makers)

### Takeaway
Every firm that describes its process publicly makes the same three points. Research starts from a hypothesis or prior and not from a blind search. The product is a large library of small, weakly correlated signals that are combined, not one winning rule. Backtest results are heavily discounted for the number of tests run. Market makers (Jane Street, Optiver, Citadel Securities) earn their edge from infrastructure, latency and flow, and a retail directional trader cannot copy that.

### Cited Findings
- **Two Sigma (documented, firm blog).** Its features research is "grounded in hypothesis formation, where you start with a prior about why something might matter and then test it. If the data doesn't confirm your prior, you don't try to rationalize it away." Two Sigma says the output is a long list of individually small signals that "like dots of a Seurat painting, start to paint a clearer picture when you put them all together." It also says the goal is to find new predictions that are meaningfully different from what already exists. — [Two Sigma, Ben Wellington / features research](https://www.twosigma.com/?p=8010); [Anything Can Be Language Now](https://www.twosigma.com/?p=7652)
- **Two Sigma treats a cleanly disproved hypothesis as a useful result.** Such a result "narrows the search space, saves time, and raises confidence in what remains." — [Why Failure Is an Option—in Science](https://www.twosigma.com/articles/why-failure-is-an-option-in-science)
- **WorldQuant (documented, own paper).** In "101 Formulaic Alphas", Kakushadze, Lauprete and Tulchinsky published 101 real production alphas, mostly price-volume based. Their average pairwise correlation is 15.9% (median 14.3%). The authors say they chose simpler alphas for the paper and that many more complex, "non-formulaic" alphas exist. A secondary summary says 80 of the 101 were in production when the paper came out. — [arXiv 1601.00991](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.00991); [hedgefundalpha summary](https://hedgefundalpha.com/strategies/101-formulaic-alphas/)
- **WorldQuant scale.** Tulchinsky's 2019 preface to *Finding Alphas* (2nd ed., Wiley 2020) says "we have produced over 20 million alphas." A 2018 Bloomberg profile describes a goal of 100 million alphas. Other divisions combine the alphas into strategies and then into larger portfolios. Outside quants submit alphas through the BRAIN platform, and the Global Alphathon competition started in 2022. — [Finding Alphas PDF](https://jchenmir.github.io/download/Finding_Alphas__A_Quantitative_Approach_to_Building_Trading_Strategies-Igor_Tulchinsky_et_al.pdf); [Bloomberg 2018](https://prod.cm.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2018-09-18/worldquant-s-tulchinsky-talks-bad-quants-100-million-alphas); [Wikipedia: WorldQuant](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldQuant)
- **Combining alphas is a separate research problem.** "How to combine a billion alphas" in the Journal of Asset Management is attributed to Kakushadze and Yu from WorldQuant-linked work, but the search snippet did not confirm the authors. — [Springer s41260-016-0004-9](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41260-016-0004-9)
- **Man AHL (documented, firm publications).** Man AHL started an Academic Advisory Board in 2014, and Campbell Harvey has been an advisor since 2005. A Man article says it is common practice to discount backtested Sharpe ratios by 50%. Harvey and Liu's "Evaluating Trading Strategies", written with input from AHL, proposes a Sharpe "haircut" that grows with the number of tests run. Harvey's slides cite AHL Research simulations of random strategies. — [Man Institute: Overfitting and its impact](https://www.man.com/maninstitute/overfitting-and-its-impact-on-the-investor); [Man: Backtesting](https://www.man.com/insights/backtesting); [Harvey & Liu, Evaluating Trading Strategies](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/206/Evaluating%20Trading%20Strategies.pdf); [Harvey Lucky Factors slides](https://jacobslevycenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/Lucky-Factors-Presentation.pdf)
- **Renaissance (anecdote via Zuckerman's book).** Robert Mercer is quoted as saying "We're right 50.75 percent of the time … but we're 100 percent right 50.75 percent of the time." The book reads this as a slight edge applied across thousands of simultaneous trades, with the law of large numbers doing the rest. Medallion is reported to have averaged about 66% a year before fees since 1988. — [Goodreads notes on Zuckerman](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/46025922-the-man-who-solved-the-market/88044824-alok-kejriwal?page=6); [Quartr on Medallion](https://quartr.com/insights/company-research/renaissance-technologies-and-the-medallion-fund); [CFA Institute review](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/blogs/enterprising-investor/2020/book-review-the-man-who-solved-the-market)
- **Market makers.** Headland Technologies (a trading firm blog) says cutting latency from 100 to 10 microseconds sharply lowers the "option-selling" cost of market making. It also says S&P futures market makers quote only when they have reliable liquid hedges elsewhere. — [Headland blog: latency competition](https://blog.headlandstech.com/?p=434). Optiver writes publicly on exchange fee and rebate structures. — [Optiver insights](https://www.optiver.com/insights/markets/the-pfof-you-didnt-know-existed-exchange-marketing-response-fees/)

### Inferences
- The professional model is a "factory": many cheap, simple, low-correlation signals, each too weak to trade on its own, combined in a portfolio layer. Selection happens at the portfolio level, judged by the marginal contribution of a new signal, and not by whether one rule passes on its own. The lab's design does the reverse. Each family must pass DSR/PBO alone, and passing is the only way forward. Under that design, a signal with a Sharpe of 0.2–0.4 and low correlation, which is exactly what WorldQuant collects, is always rejected.
- WorldQuant and Two Sigma can absorb millions of weak alphas because their costs are cross-sectional and institutional, spread across thousands of stocks. A single-instrument intraday futures lab cannot diversify that way. Its breadth is limited to a handful of markets (WIN, WDO, maybe DI, BGI, CCM) times a few time horizons.
- Every firm that has spoken about short-horizon futures edges (market makers, HFT) ties them to latency and flow. A retail MT5 demo connection has neither. So "intraday directional edge on WIN from bar data" is the part of the strategy space with the most competition.

### Gaps
- I found no public, detailed account of how Citadel, Jane Street or Optiver run their research workflow (review gates, sign-off). Their public material is about recruiting or market structure.
- I found no primary WorldQuant description of how BRAIN submissions are scored and combined. The BRAIN numbers come from Wikipedia and press coverage.
- I did not retrieve Renaissance details beyond the Zuckerman book. The firm publishes nothing about its process.

## 2. What successful independent and small systematic traders report

### Takeaway
Independent practitioners who make money live give the same advice. Start from an economic reason for the edge (who is on the other side, and why), and not from a rule search. Trade many simple, diversified rules with modest, handcrafted weights. Size by volatility. Hold positions long enough that costs are a small fraction of expected gross edge. Incubate for months before risking money. Several of them warn explicitly against the generate-and-filter approach ("data mining", "vibe quanting").

### Cited Findings
- **Robot Wealth, Kris Longmore (blog, documented opinion).** He calls building backtest frameworks and ML systems "before I even understood what edge I was trying to capture … completely backwards." He tells traders to form small hypotheses, ask what the data should show if each one is true, and "design experiments to challenge your hypotheses." In "Why Data Mining Risks Your Trading Career" he writes: "Feed enough data into a computer, run enough tests, and surely something profitable will emerge, right? Maybe. But almost certainly not." He accepts data mining only when the parameter space is tightly constrained by market knowledge. In a recent piece he calls AI-generated rule search ("vibe quanting") essentially data mining, and says the backtest "sits above and outside of the edge" and is a poor tool for understanding it. Robot Wealth's framing: "Real research starts with the mechanism: who's on the other side of your trade, and why they're willing to pay you." — [Confessions of a recovering engineer (IBKR repost)](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/ibkr-quant-news/confessions-of-a-recovering-engineer-or-why-engineers-make-bad-traders/); [Why data mining risks your trading career](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/ibkr-quant-news/why-data-mining-risks-your-trading-career/); [Robot Wealth](https://robotwealth.com); [Conversation with Robot Wealth pt. 3](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/ibkr-quant-news/a-conversation-with-robot-wealth-part-three-ideas-and-edges-rules-based-and-collaborative-systematic-trading/)
- **Robert Carver (book and blog; partly secondary).** His "speed limit" rule says trading costs should be at most one third of a conservative estimate of a system's pre-cost Sharpe ratio. Since most subsystems have a pre-cost Sharpe below 0.4, that means an annual cost budget of about 0.13 Sharpe units. Carver measures costs "as a negative Sharpe Ratio." He says expensive instruments can still perform well after costs, but only when traded slowly. — [CXO Advisory notes on Systematic Trading](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/a-few-notes-on-systematic-trading); [the7circles: Systematic Trading 5 – Speed](https://the7circles.uk/?p=24243); [Carver: How fast should we trade (2020)](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2020/04/how-fast-should-we-trade.html)
- **Carver, "Fast but not furious" (2023).** His choice of which rule variations each instrument uses is "primarily based around costs." He only trades when the position moves outside a buffer around the ideal position. Buffering cuts turnover by about 5% for the fastest EWMAC rule and about 30% for the slowest. — [qoppac 2023/02](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2023/02/fast-but-not-furious-do-fast-trading.html) (opening section only, from the search snippet)
- **Ernie Chan (2009 interview, primary).** "I stick to the simplest and the most basic strategies. I find that to be profitable." He chose simple strategies because an independent trader cannot run what banks run. Secondary summaries of his books say independents should look for niches too small or messy for large firms, and should size at half-Kelly. Verify those against the books. — [Stocks & Commodities interview 2009](https://www.traders.com/Documentation/FEEDbk_Docs/2009/07/Interview.html); [O'Reilly: Quantitative Trading ch. 8](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/quantitative-trading-2nd/9781119800064/c08.xhtml)
- **Kevin Davey (podcast and secondary summaries).** He calls his method a "process": an idea, then a limited backtest, then walk-forward, then Monte Carlo, then incubation, then live. He runs many simple systems rather than one. His incubation lengths are covered in section 5. — [Algo Advantage 036: It's all about process](https://algoadvantage.substack.com/p/036-kevin-davey-part-i-its-all-about); [037: Selecting strategies](https://algoadvantage.substack.com/p/037-kevin-davey-ii-selecting-optimal); [forex.in.rs summary (secondary)](https://www.forex.in.rs/simple-systems-diversified)

### Inferences
- Applied to WIN/WDO, Carver's speed limit is a hard filter the lab can compute before generating anything. With a realistic gross Sharpe of 0.3–0.5 for a simple rule, the cost budget is about 0.1–0.17 Sharpe units a year. A 1-minute rule with hundreds of round trips a year on WIN at R$-per-contract costs almost certainly uses up that budget. The owner's preference of at least 500 trades a year per market pushes the lab straight into the worst part of the cost/edge trade-off. This matches the lab's own note that WDO is "unfair at R$30/30."
- None of the independents describe a 26,000-variant search. Their idea flow is tens of hypotheses a year, each tied to a mechanism.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve primary text from Euan Sinclair, Tomas Nesnidal, Andreas Clenow, Quantitativo or Darwinex in this pass. The search for Sinclair returned irrelevant results. The report writer should not attribute specific claims to them from these notes.
- I found no audited live track records for these independents. Their success is self-reported, apart from Carver's prior institutional career at AHL and Davey's results in public trading contests, which I did not verify here.

## 3. Evidence on small-capital intraday retail outcomes, and what the profitable minority does differently

### Takeaway
The best data sets show that sustained profitable retail day trading is rare: under 1% in Taiwan and about 1% earning above the minimum wage in Brazil's mini-index futures. The Brazilian study covers exactly the instrument this lab trades. The profitable minority does show persistent skill. But most people do not learn, and losing traders keep trading.

### Cited Findings
- **Brazil, Chague, De-Losso and Giovannetti, "Day Trading for a Living?" (2019/2020).** The data are from the CVM (Brazil's securities regulator) and cover all 19,646 individuals who started day trading **mini-Ibovespa futures** (the WIN contract) in 2013–2015. Among those who persisted more than 300 days, about 97% lost money. Only about 1.1% earned more than the Brazilian minimum wage, and about 0.4–0.5% earned more than a bank teller's starting salary (sources differ on 0.4 vs 0.5). The authors found no evidence of learning. Caveat: the sample period may have been especially hard for index futures. — [SSRN 3423101](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101); [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/fgv/eesptd/525.html); [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/retail-day-trading-is-an-uphill-battle/); [CXO Advisory caveat](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=32819)
- **Brazil follow-up (2025).** The COVID-19 pandemic pushed the number of Brazilian day traders to about 100,000 active participants a day. — [Chague & Giovannetti, Brazilian Review of Finance](https://periodicos.fgv.br/rbfin/article/view/94291)
- **Taiwan, Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean, "The Cross-Section of Speculator Skill" (J. Financial Markets 2014).** The study uses complete Taiwan Stock Exchange data for 1992–2006. Ranked by prior-year returns, the top 500 day traders earned about 61 bps a day before fees and 38 bps after fees in the following year. The bottom-ranked traders earned −11.5 bps a day before fees and −28.9 bps after. Less than 1% of day traders predictably and reliably earn positive abnormal returns net of fees. — [Barber et al. 2014 (eScholarship)](https://www.escholarship.org/content/qt7k75v0qx/qt7k75v0qx.pdf?t=ohrd1z); [RePEc entry](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:18:y:2014:i:c:p:1-24)
- **Taiwan, "Learning, Fast or Slow" (Rev. Asset Pricing Studies 2020).** Aggregate day-trader performance net of fees was negative in every one of the 15 years. Unprofitable traders are more likely to quit, and more than 75–80% quit within two years. Still, 74% of day-trading volume comes from traders with a history of losses. The authors read this as overconfidence and biased learning. — [RAPS via RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rasset:v:10:y:2020:i:1:p:61-93.); [Working paper PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf)

### Inferences
- The base rate for "profitable intraday WIN trading by an individual" is about 1%. These were mostly discretionary humans, not systematic labs, so the rate is not a ceiling for a disciplined systematic process. It is still the right prior. A lab finding no validated edge in 26,000 variants is consistent with that prior, and is evidence that the lab's gates work, not that its process failed.
- Persistence among Taiwan's top traders shows that skill exists at the top. The published papers do not say what those traders did: these data sets do not record strategies. The likely explanations are liquidity provision with limit orders, speed and information, but the Barber papers do not establish this. Treat it as a gap.

### Gaps
- I found no academic study of systematic (algorithmic) retail traders' live outcomes on B3. All the Brazil evidence covers individuals in general.
- I could not confirm the exact Chague percentages from the full text: the RePEc fetch was blocked, and secondary sources give 0.4% vs 0.5%.

## 4. Signal combination and strategy portfolios vs single-rule selection

### Takeaway
Professionals and the best-documented independents combine many forecasts. They use fixed or handcrafted weights and correlation-aware scaling (Carver's forecast diversification multiplier), or factor and alpha combination at scale (WorldQuant). They do not select a single winner. Combining weak, low-correlation signals raises the portfolio Sharpe roughly with the square root of effective breadth, and it reduces the selection bias that a single-winner search makes worse.

### Cited Findings
- **Carver, forecast combination.** Each trading rule produces a forecast scaled to an average absolute value of 10 and capped at ±20. Forecasts are combined with weights, then multiplied by a forecast diversification multiplier (FDM) that rescales the combined forecast back to the target average. The FDM corrects for the drop in volatility that comes from averaging imperfectly correlated forecasts. In one worked example: target volatility 10 / combined volatility 8.66 gives an FDM of about 1.15. Weights are set by "handcrafting": group similar rules bottom-up and use correlations, without full optimisation. pysystemtrade implements this in its `ForecastCombine` stage and can estimate weights and multipliers from config. — [QuantConnect: Combined carry and trend (Carver method)](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/16001/combined-carry-and-trend/); [the7circles: Frameworks and Forecasts](https://the7circles.uk/?p=24066); [qoppac 2018 handcrafting posts](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2018); [pysystemtrade backtesting docs](https://github.com/vcaldas/pysystemtrade/blob/master/docs/backtesting.md)
- **WorldQuant.** Low pairwise correlation (about 16%) among simple alphas is what makes combining them worth it, and it is the selection criterion WorldQuant publishes alongside the formulas. — [arXiv 1601.00991](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.00991)
- **Two Sigma.** Its stated selection criterion is whether a new signal is "meaningfully different from what already exists", not whether it is the best on its own. — [Two Sigma](https://www.twosigma.com/?p=7652)

### Inferences
- Concrete redirect for the lab: change the unit of approval from "one family passes DSR/PBO alone" to "a pre-registered portfolio of N simple, mechanism-motivated rules with fixed or handcrafted weights passes as one strategy." This is one test, not N, so the multiple-testing penalty drops sharply. Add new rules on the basis of low correlation with the existing book and a positive marginal contribution, as Two Sigma and WorldQuant describe. The portfolio should still be held to Carver's cost budget.
- Breadth on B3 is limited. WIN and WDO are highly correlated with risk-on/risk-off moves, so the effective number of independent bets may be small. Diversifying across horizons (intraday plus multi-day trend and carry on DI, WDO and the index) is probably worth more than adding more intraday 1-minute variants.

### Gaps
- I found no published head-to-head study of "ensemble of rules vs best single selected rule" on intraday futures out of sample. The case rests on theory (diversification, selection bias) and on how practitioners work.

## 5. Incubation and paper-trading practice: duration, metrics, kill criteria

### Takeaway
Practitioners incubate for about 3 to 12 months, most often 6. They compare live results against the backtest's distribution (from walk-forward and Monte Carlo) and shelve a system when live equity leaves that distribution. They are explicit that incubation mostly catches implementation and plumbing errors, and that a few months is statistically too short to prove a modest edge.

### Cited Findings
- **Kevin Davey.** His sequence is walk-forward, then Monte Carlo, then incubation (live signals with no capital), then live trading. Reported incubation lengths vary: 6–12 months in one podcast summary, 3–6 months in another, and 6–9 months in a third. His Strategy Factory Club uses a "6 month live performance test" that not all submitted strategies pass. A system is shelved if its incubation equity curve diverges materially from its walk-forward curve. His book *Building Winning Algorithmic Trading Systems* has a chapter "Monte Carlo Testing and Incubation", but I could not read the full text. — [Algo Advantage 036](https://algoadvantage.substack.com/p/036-kevin-davey-part-i-its-all-about); [IBKR Campus repost](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/ibkr-quant-news/algo-advantage-036-kevin-davey-part-i-its-all-about-process-in-algo-trading/); [Student strategies doc (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/419869235/StudentStrategies-pdf); [Book TOC (studylib)](https://studylib.net/doc/27728503/kevin-davey---building-winning-algorithmic-trading-systems)
- **Incubation has limited statistical power.** A few months of paper trading holds few trades. It catches plumbing problems but struggles to separate a modest edge from luck. — [LuxAlgo: forward/paper-trading confirmation](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/forward-paper-trading-confirmation.md)
- **Quantopian, Wiecki et al. 2016, "All that Glitters Is Not Gold".** The study covers 888 strategies backtested over 2010–2015, each with 6–12 months of true out-of-sample live paper performance. In-sample performance explained only about 1–2% of out-of-sample performance (R² about 0.01–0.02). That figure comes from the CXO summary; check it against the paper. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/in-sample-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-888-trading-strategies); [Quantopian archive thread](https://quantopian-archive.netlify.app/forum/threads/q-paper-all-that-glitters-is-not-gold-comparing-backtest-and-out-of-sample-performance-on-a-large-cohort-of-trading-algorithms)
- **Man.** Its rule of thumb is to discount backtested Sharpe ratios by 50% before forming expectations for live trading. — [Man: Backtesting](https://www.man.com/insights/backtesting)

### Inferences
- The lab's rule "demo is not evidence" matches practice. Incubation is for checking fidelity: whether live fills, slippage and signals match the simulation. Useful metrics are signal agreement between live and backtest, realized vs assumed cost per trade, and trade count vs expected (the lab's D1 rule). Kill criteria should be set ahead of time: live P&L below the 5th percentile of the Monte Carlo band, or realized costs above modeled costs.
- At 500 or more trades a year, six months of demo gives about 250 trades. That is enough to estimate realized costs and slippage precisely, which is the most valuable thing a demo can tell an intraday lab. It is not enough to confirm a Sharpe of 0.5.

### Gaps
- I found no primary institutional source (AHL, Two Sigma) that gives a specific incubation length or kill rule. Firms say they use "paper portfolios" or "staged capital" but publish no numbers.

## 6. AI-agent-driven alpha research (RD-Agent, AlphaAgent): reported results and failure modes (brief)

### Takeaway
The best-known agent frameworks report positive results on daily, cross-sectional equity data (CSI 300/500, S&P 500) with a 2021–2024 test period. The claims are the authors' own, and I found no independent replication. These frameworks build in originality and complexity regularizers and use factor-library combination. They do not use single-rule search on one futures instrument, which is the lab's setup.

### Cited Findings
- **Microsoft RD-Agent(Q)** (arXiv 2505.15155, NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks track). It loops through a research stage (hypotheses), a development stage (the Co-STEER code agent), and a feedback stage, with a multi-armed bandit scheduler choosing between factor and model work. The authors report up to 2x higher annualized returns than classical factor libraries using 70% fewer factors, and a cost under $10 per run (v1). — [arXiv 2505.15155](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2505.15155); [NeurIPS 2025](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ac5c2b6e423883cbcacbcccf88491b78-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html); [GitHub microsoft/RD-Agent](https://github.com/microsoft/RD-Agent)
- **AlphaAgent** (KDD 2025, arXiv 2502.16789). It targets alpha decay and factor homogenization with three regularizers: AST-similarity originality enforcement against existing alphas, complexity control (expression-tree size and parameter count), and hypothesis-factor alignment checked by an LLM. Reported results after costs for 2021–2024: 11.0% annual excess return (IR 1.5) on CSI 500 and 8.74% (IR 1.05) on S&P 500, an 81% higher effective-factor hit ratio, and 30% fewer tokens. A later third-party comparison (AlphaCrafter) reports AlphaAgent's IC holding at about 0.02. — [arXiv 2502.16789](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16789v2); [AlphaCrafter arXiv 2605.05580](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05580)
- **Failure modes the papers name themselves:** "crowded signals and accelerated decay" from homogenized LLM factors (AlphaAgent). Practitioners add that AI rule generation without a mechanism is data mining ("vibe quanting"). — [AlphaAgent](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16789v2); [Robot Wealth (via IBKR)](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/ibkr-quant-news/why-data-mining-risks-your-trading-career/)

### Inferences
- The lab's agents rewrite the same price-volume patterns on one or two instruments. That is exactly the homogenization these papers penalize. Adopting AST-similarity and complexity limits, plus counting every variant as a trial (which the lab already does), would make fewer and more distinct proposals.
- The published successes rely on cross-sectional breadth (hundreds of stocks) at daily frequency, where weak factors can be combined. There is no evidence that these frameworks find edges in single-instrument intraday futures. Moving the agents to a breadth-rich universe, or to multi-day horizons on several B3 futures, matches the setting where they have been shown to work.

### Gaps
- I found no independent, out-of-sample replication of the RD-Agent(Q) or AlphaAgent headline returns. I also found no evidence of these frameworks producing live trading P&L.
